# Product specification (as built)

See the package brief for the full original scope. This file records what shipped. The one-line promise is "Check the seller's story before you buy the business"; the first session should let a buyer answer what is claimed, what the evidence says, what could change their judgment, and what to ask the seller.

- Routes: `/`, `/demo`, `/methodology`, `/github`, `/app`, `/app/deals/new`, `/app/deals/[id]` (+ `documents`, `claims`, `financials`, `scenarios`, `report`, `audit`).
- Demo: `/demo` gives each visitor their own demo user and their own seeded copy of the Northstar deal; a signed-in account keeps its identity. Visitors cannot see each other's deals. A visitor's demo identity, deals, and files are removed 14 days after their newest session expired.
- Deal creation fields: company, industry, purchase price and basis, date, debt, equity, rate, amortization, covenant threshold (plus debt assumed / cash acquired for the equity-price basis).
- Inputs: PDF CIM, PDF term sheet, PDF contracts, XLSX statements (a sheet named like an income statement: `Income Statement`, `P&L`, `Profit and Loss`), XLSX model, CSV customer revenue. Limits: 25 MB per file, 300 PDF pages, 20,000 sheet rows, 50,000 CSV rows, an XLSX archive budget, 50 documents per deal, 500 MB per user.
- Claim types: revenue, revenue growth, customer concentration, recurring revenue, churn, adjusted EBITDA, add-back, gross/operating margin, margin improvement, forecast, contract term, debt term, one-time expense.
- Statuses: supported, contradicted, unsupported, review required (+ pending before verification). The claim page labels the numbers "Seller says" and "Documents show", explains what confirming a status means, and says confidence is the extractor's confidence that the sentence is a claim, not that it is true.
- Review actions: confirm assessment (API verb `accept`), reject (note required), correct (value/unit/status + note), undo. Add-back decisions with rationale.
- Findings: written for the buyer. The title states what is wrong with formatted figures, the detail says what it means and names the document it was checked against; ids stay in the evidence and metric links. One engine formatter produces every reader-facing number.
- Deal overview: a start section above the cards that names the next step in plain words (review the claims, or add documents when the deal is empty), a secondary action to test a downside scenario, and a short glossary (adjusted EBITDA, DSCR) behind a disclosure; then the verification summary, adjusted EBITDA comparison, DSCR by scenario, top findings, missing information, documents, and latest report cards.
- Financial verification: "What changed and why" first (seller's adjusted EBITDA, verified figure, difference, reported EBITDA, excluded adjustments with reasons), then the add-back bridge, deal metrics with a one-line meaning each, CFADS and DSCR, and the mapped income statement last.
- Citation viewer: sheet evidence as one aligned table per sheet with the workbook header, row numbers, thousands separators, and cited rows highlighted and announced.
- Scenarios: base, downside, severe downside, custom; twelve assumptions with ranges and one plain sentence of help each; a sticky result strip keeps year-one DSCR against the covenant visible while editing; immutable runs; sensitivity grid.
- Report: fourteen sections, four outcomes, validation, Markdown and PDF export.
- Ask the deal (chat): streaming, per-deal threads, tool-use loop over persisted rows for Anthropic and any OpenAI-compatible provider, no-tools fallback, rule-based composer without a key, model picker, copy and regenerate, scope label per reply, citation markers checked in code (provenance, not truth). The panel is `min(80vw, 880px)` wide on desktop and full width on mobile; four quick prompts sit under the composer (explain this finding, what should I ask the seller, what is missing, what changed after this scenario); "Ask about this claim" and "Ask" on an add-back hand the claim or adjustment into the chat as plain text; a metric cited twice collapses to a "same metric" marker; the footer says how many citations resolve and asks for review.
- Landing page: a plain-language framing section ("The seller's story, checked line by line against the paperwork.") with the four-step first session; the Evidence Core scene told in three chapters (seller says 18% growth, the statements show 11.6%, open the evidence) with visible captions and a pause control; reduced motion and no-WebGL get the static composition automatically. The close section's counts come from the committed snapshot.
- Optional features implemented: add-back bridge, sensitivity matrix, downloadable report, citation-first Q&A ("Ask the Deal", `POST /deals/{id}/ask`, ⌘/ or Ctrl+/ in the app), one-click citation navigation, document viewer with inert-instruction labeling.
- Security posture: per-visitor demo isolation with retention, bounded upload reads, XLSX archive budget, storage quotas, per-user token-bucket rate limits on demo start, upload, reprocess, process, chat, and ask. See `SECURITY.md` for what is still missing.
- Deal Room: three panels, what you uploaded (plain document types, what was read from each file, size, status, technical details behind a disclosure), what we understood, and what is missing (expected document types with a one-line purpose each).
- Audit history: a plain sentence per event, grouped filters, the viewer's time zone named, payloads behind a disclosure.
- Deal creation: blank required numbers are errors, never zero; equity-basis fields appear only for that basis; server errors shown as a plain sentence naming the field.
- Report page: reading column capped at 76 characters, evidence chips open the source at every breakpoint, prose rules enforced by a test (no ids in text, formatted numbers, figure-free section intros).
- Not implemented: reviewer comments UI (table exists), version comparison, claim graph, IC checklist, manual statement mapping, OCR for scanned PDFs, document deletion through the API, retention for non-demo uploads, a shared rate limiter across API processes. What remains from the 2026-09-06 review is listed in `docs/astra-review-response.md`.
