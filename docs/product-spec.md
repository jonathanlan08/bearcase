# Product specification (as built)

See the package brief for the full original scope. This file records what shipped. The one-line promise is "BearCase checks a seller's documents for financial inconsistencies and shows you what to investigate before buying the business." The first customer is a person buying their first small business with an accountant or adviser; small acquisition teams are the second segment (`docs/go-to-market.md`). The product does one workflow: upload documents, see the important discrepancies, inspect the evidence, export questions for the seller. The first session should let a buyer answer what is claimed, what the evidence says, what could change their judgment, and what to ask the seller.

- Routes: `/`, `/demo`, `/methodology`, `/trust`, `/github`, `/app`, `/app/deals/new`, `/app/deals/[id]` (+ `documents`, `claims`, `financials`, `scenarios`, `report`, `questions`, `audit`).
- Demo: `/demo` gives each visitor their own demo user and their own seeded copy of the Northstar deal; a signed-in account keeps its identity. Visitors cannot see each other's deals. A visitor's demo identity, deals, and files are removed 14 days after their newest session expired.
- Deal creation fields: company, industry, purchase price and basis, date, debt, equity, rate, amortization, covenant threshold (plus debt assumed / cash acquired for the equity-price basis).
- Inputs: PDF CIM, PDF term sheet, PDF contracts, XLSX statements (a sheet named like an income statement: `Income Statement`, `P&L`, `Profit and Loss`), XLSX model, CSV customer revenue. Limits: 25 MB per file, 300 PDF pages, 20,000 sheet rows, 50,000 CSV rows, an XLSX archive budget, 50 documents per deal, 500 MB per user.
- Claim types: revenue, revenue growth, customer concentration, recurring revenue, churn, adjusted EBITDA, add-back, gross/operating margin, margin improvement, forecast, contract term, debt term, one-time expense.
- Statuses: supported, contradicted, unsupported, review required (+ pending before verification). The claim page labels the numbers "Seller says" and "Documents show", explains what confirming a status means, and says confidence is the extractor's confidence that the sentence is a claim, not that it is true.
- Review actions: confirm assessment (API verb `accept`), reject (note required), correct (value/unit/status + note), undo. Add-back decisions with rationale.
- Findings: written for the buyer. The title states what is wrong with formatted figures, the detail says what it means and names the document it was checked against; ids stay in the evidence and metric links. One engine formatter produces every reader-facing number.
- Deal overview: a progress strip with the four workflow steps (documents, findings, evidence, questions), each done or not, each linking to its page (`GET /api/deals/{id}/progress`; documents is done once a document is processed, findings once a reviewer decision or a chat message exists in the deal, evidence once a source was opened, questions once the seller questions were exported); a start section above the cards that names the next step in plain words (review the claims, or add documents when the deal is empty), a secondary action to test a downside scenario, and a short glossary (adjusted EBITDA, DSCR) behind a disclosure; then the verification summary, adjusted EBITDA comparison, DSCR by scenario, top findings, missing information, documents, and latest report cards.
- Questions for the seller (`/app/deals/[id]/questions`, `GET /api/deals/{id}/seller-questions`): one question per finding or unresolved claim, with why it matters, its kind (contradiction, unsupported, missing document, covenant, concentration, risk, integrity), severity, the evidence and metric ids, the claim or finding it came from, and the document names; deterministic, built by one shared function in `reports/assemble.py` that the report's "Management questions" section also uses, so the page and the report never disagree. `GET .../seller-questions/export?format=md|txt` downloads the list (Content-Disposition) and records `seller_questions.exported` with the count. Each question, and each finding on the overview, offers "Explain this finding" (prefills the chat) and "Draft a question for the seller about this" (sends, asking for the evidence ids to be cited) through `lib/chat-bus.ts`. The page is the sixth primary navigation item, "Seller Questions", after the report, and the `/trust` page is linked from the rail and the mobile menu as "Trust & data".
- Trust page (`/trust`, "How BearCase handles your documents"): plain language for a first-time buyer on where documents are stored and who can read them, what deletion removes, retention (demo uploads removed 14 days after the visitor's newest session expires; a signed-in account's uploads kept until the owner deletes them; no automatic retention yet), which provider receives what (extraction sees document text when live; the chat sends claim text and evidence snippets; the provider and model are named in the panel), and the four kinds of statement the product tells apart: source facts, calculations, assumptions, and AI interpretations. It says that a resolving citation shows provenance and does not establish that an answer is correct.
- Usage panel (audit history page, `GET /api/deals/{id}/usage`): chat messages, input and output tokens, tool calls, and a count per model; document count, bytes, pages, and rows; stored bytes; a cost estimate in USD from the per-model price table in `chat/providers.py` (free tiers priced at zero; an unknown model gives no cost) with the pricing note shown beside it. It is an estimate for tracking pilots (`docs/go-to-market.md`), not an invoice.
- Document deletion (`DELETE /api/deals/{id}/documents/{document_id}`, 204, owner-scoped): removes the document, its versions and stored files, its evidence, and the claims whose source is that document, with their links and decisions; records `document.deleted`; the `X-BearCase-Reanalyse: true` response header tells the UI to suggest "Run analysis" because findings and metrics may be stale.
- Review dataset (`GET /api/deals/{id}/review-dataset`, owner only, records `review_dataset.exported`): a JSONL download with one line per claim that has at least one reviewer decision, carrying the claim, the AI's status, rule, and rationale, the reviewer's action, status, value, and note, and the linked evidence (text capped at 200 characters). The CLI equivalents are `bearcase export-reviews` and `bearcase eval --reviews`; `docs/evaluation.md` explains the agreement metric and the release gate.
- Financial verification: "What changed and why" first (seller's adjusted EBITDA, verified figure, difference, reported EBITDA, excluded adjustments with reasons), then the add-back bridge, deal metrics with a one-line meaning each, CFADS and DSCR, and the mapped income statement last.
- Citation viewer: sheet evidence as one aligned table per sheet with the workbook header, row numbers, thousands separators, and cited rows highlighted and announced.
- Scenarios: base, downside, severe downside, custom; twelve assumptions with ranges and one plain sentence of help each; a sticky result strip keeps year-one DSCR against the covenant visible while editing; immutable runs; sensitivity grid.
- Report: fourteen sections, four outcomes, validation, Markdown and PDF export.
- Ask the deal (chat): streaming, per-deal threads, tool-use loop over persisted rows for Anthropic and any OpenAI-compatible provider, no-tools fallback, rule-based composer without a key, model picker, copy and regenerate, scope label per reply, citation markers checked in code (provenance, not truth). The panel is `min(80vw, 880px)` wide on desktop and full width on mobile; four quick prompts sit under the composer (explain this finding, what should I ask the seller, what is missing, what changed after this scenario); "Ask about this claim" and "Ask" on an add-back hand the claim or adjustment into the chat as plain text; a metric cited twice collapses to a "same metric" marker; the footer says how many citations resolve and asks for review.
- Landing page: a plain-language framing section ("The seller's story, checked line by line against the paperwork.") with the four-step first session; the Evidence Sculpture scene told in three chapters (seller says 18% growth, the statements show 11.6%, open the evidence) with visible captions and a pause control; reduced motion and no-WebGL get the static composition automatically. The close section's counts come from the committed snapshot.
- Optional features implemented: add-back bridge, sensitivity matrix, downloadable report, citation-first Q&A ("Ask the Deal", `POST /deals/{id}/ask`, ⌘/ or Ctrl+/ in the app), one-click citation navigation, document viewer with inert-instruction labeling.
- Security posture: per-visitor demo isolation with retention, bounded upload reads, XLSX archive budget, storage quotas, per-user token-bucket rate limits on demo start, upload, reprocess, process, chat, and ask. See `SECURITY.md` for what is still missing.
- Deal Room: three panels, what you uploaded (plain document types, what was read from each file, size, status, technical details behind a disclosure), what we understood, and what is missing (expected document types with a one-line purpose each).
- Audit history: a plain sentence per event, grouped filters, the viewer's time zone named, payloads behind a disclosure. Events added for the workflow: `evidence.opened` (recorded when a user opens `GET /api/deals/{id}/evidence/{evidence_id}`, at most one row per evidence per user per day), `seller_questions.exported`, `document.deleted`, `review_dataset.exported`.
- Deal creation: blank required numbers are errors, never zero; equity-basis fields appear only for that basis; server errors shown as a plain sentence naming the field.
- Report page: reading column capped at 76 characters, evidence chips open the source at every breakpoint, prose rules enforced by a test (no ids in text, formatted numbers, figure-free section intros).
- Not implemented: reviewer comments UI (table exists), version comparison, claim graph, IC checklist, manual statement mapping, OCR for scanned PDFs, automatic retention for non-demo uploads (owners delete documents themselves), a shared rate limiter across API processes, a per-deal permission flag for the reviewed-work set (permission is recorded by hand for now). What remains from the 2026-09-06 review is listed in `docs/astra-review-response.md`; the customer-facing plan and its measures are in `docs/go-to-market.md` and `docs/evaluation.md`.
